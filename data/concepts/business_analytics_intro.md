## 1. Ground Truth Invariants

### Invariant 1: Definition of Business Analytics
- Business analytics is the science of manipulating data using models and statistical formulae to find insights that solve business-related problems.
- It is a subset of methodologies including data mining, statistical analysis, and predictive analytics.

### Invariant 2: Core Components of Business Analytics
- **Data Storage**: The process of storing data for future use, utilizing products like object storage and block storage.
- **Data Visualization**: Graphically representing information or insights drawn from data analysis.
- **Insights**: Outputs and inferences derived from data analysis using business analytics techniques and tools.
- **Data Security**: Monitoring and identifying malicious activities in security networks using real-time data and predictive modeling techniques.

### Invariant 3: Types of Business Analytics
- **Descriptive Analytics**: Summarizing past data to answer questions about what has occurred before.
- **Predictive Analytics**: Making predictions about future events based on historical data.
- **Prescriptive Analytics**: Using simulation, data modeling, and optimization algorithms to find answers to "what needs to be done."

### Invariant 4: Analytics Life Cycle
- **Business Understanding**: Formulating project objectives and requirements from a business perspective.
- **Data Understanding**: Initial data collection and familiarization, identifying data quality problems, and discovering first insights.
- **Data Preparation**: Constructing the final dataset from initial raw data.
- **Modeling**: Evaluating, selecting, and applying appropriate modeling techniques.
- **Evaluation**: Building and choosing models that appear to have high quality and validating that they cover all key business issues.

## 2. Common Fallacy Patterns (Known Misconceptions)

### `PREDICTIVE_EQUALS_PRESCRIPTIVE`
- **Description:** Students conflate predictive analytics with prescriptive analytics, believing that forecasting future outcomes is the same as recommending specific actions to take.
- **Example flawed claim:** *Since our predictive model forecasts a drop in sales next quarter, it already tells us we should cut costs and hire fewer staff.*
- **Pedagogical counter:** If a weather forecast predicts rain tomorrow, does the forecast itself tell you whether to carry an umbrella, cancel a picnic, or install a drainage system — or does deciding among those options require a separate step?

### `LIFECYCLE_IS_LINEAR`
- **Description:** Students assume the analytics life cycle proceeds in a strict one-directional sequence from business understanding through evaluation, never returning to earlier phases.
- **Example flawed claim:** *Once we finish the evaluation phase and pick the best model, the analytics life cycle is complete and we move on to a completely new project.*
- **Pedagogical counter:** If your evaluation reveals that the model doesn't cover a key business issue you initially overlooked, would you really start a brand-new project from scratch instead of revisiting your business understanding or data preparation steps?

### `VISUALIZATION_IS_INSIGHT`
- **Description:** Students mistake data visualization (graphically representing data) for insights (inferences derived from analysis), believing that producing a chart equals discovering actionable knowledge.
- **Example flawed claim:** *I created a bar chart showing monthly revenue, so I have derived an insight that will help solve the company's business problem.*
- **Pedagogical counter:** If you plot your company's daily stock prices on a line graph, have you discovered why the price changed, or have you merely displayed the raw values in a visual format — what additional step is needed to turn that display into an insight?
## 3. Lecture Notes

Business analytics is the process of manipulating data to find insights that solve business-related problems. It involves using various tools and methodologies, such as data mining, statistical analysis, and predictive analytics, to transform data into useful information. The core components of business analytics include data storage, data visualization, insights, and data security. Data storage involves storing data for future use, while data visualization graphically represents information or insights drawn from data analysis. Insights are the outputs and inferences derived from data analysis, and data security involves monitoring and identifying malicious activities in security networks.

There are three main types of business analytics: descriptive, predictive, and prescriptive. **Descriptive analytics** summarizes past data to answer questions about what has occurred before. **Predictive analytics** makes predictions about future events based on historical data. **Prescriptive analytics** uses simulation, data modeling, and optimization algorithms to find answers to "what needs to be done." The analytics life cycle includes business understanding, data understanding, data preparation, modeling, and evaluation. Each phase focuses on different aspects of the data analysis process, ensuring that the final models are of high quality and cover all key business issues.
